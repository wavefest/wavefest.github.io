The Women Amplify the Voice of Electroacoustic music (WAVE) festival is the first women-focused electroacoustic music festival. The festival's mission is to support, showcase, and celebrate women in electronic music and art disciplines.  As a key underrepresented group in this genre's history of predominantly male, European contributions, this festival provides a unique opportunity and a central stage to showcase the creativity and brilliance of women's contributions.
This year's festival is partially sponsored by SEAMUS Micro CREATE Grant and University of Oregon School of Music and Dance CEI Mini Grant. 

# Staff

## Director: Li Tao

<img markdown="0" src="{{ site.baseurl }}/img/Tao.png" width=210 class="bio-img"/>

Award winning composer Li Tao 李陶 was born and raised in Beijing, China and currently based in the U.S. teaching in the Music Technology area at the University of Oregon School of Music and Dance, where she earned both of her Ph.D.s in Music Composition and Data-Driven Music Performance & Composition. The philosophy, literature, and spirituality of the ancient East play a formative role in the aesthetic of Tao’s work. Her music consists of vivid soundscapes, colorful timbres, and interdisciplinary elements that often lead her audiences on a multi-dimensional journey full of imagination. As an Asian female musician, Tao is devoted to promoting gender equity and cultural diversity through her music as well as through collaboration with other artists.

Tao’s music has been performed at concerts and music festivals throughout the world including China, Japan, Korea, Australia, Ireland, Belgium, Ukraine, Brazil, and the U.S.A. Her primary interests include acoustic and electroacoustic composition, performance practices, and analysis of compositional techniques, aesthetics, and intercultural dialogues.

## Assistant Director: Oliver Kwapis

<img markdown="0" src="{{ site.baseurl }}/img/Oliver.jpg" width=300 class="bio-img"/>

Oliver Kwapis (b. 1997) is a composer, performer, and teacher of electroacoustic and acoustic music. His creative practice is informed by questions of identity, memory, and embodiment. He often uses music to engage with his family history, personal and cultural memory, and his relationship with the natural world. Many of his works incorporate original and found texts, which he uses either as primary vehicles for meaning or as sonic material within more abstract compositions.

His electroacoustic music has been featured at conferences and festivals including the International Computer Music Conference (ICMC), Society for Electro-Acoustic Music in the United States National Conference (SEAMUS), New Interfaces for Musical Expression (NIME), New York City Electroacoustic Music Festival (NYCEMF), MISE-EN FESTIVAL, and the National Student Electronic Music Event (NSEME). He has also created sound for installations held at the LaVerne Krause Gallery at the University of Oregon and the Grunwald Gallery of Art at Indiana University.

He is currently a Visiting Lecturer in Music in the Department of Composition (Electronic and Computer Music) at the Indiana University Jacobs School of Music. He holds a PhD in Data-driven Music Performance and Composition from the University of Oregon School of Music and Dance, where he studied with Jeffrey Stolet and served as a Graduate Employee in the Department of Music Technology. He also holds degrees from the Oberlin Conservatory of Music and the Indiana University Jacobs School of Music.
